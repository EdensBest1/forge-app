## 1. Executive objective

Build a controlled enterprise-acquisition and workforce-fulfillment engine that uses the 60-agent Nexus registry to:

1. research and maintain a verified enterprise prospect database;
2. identify potential blue-collar workforce, subcontractor, field-service, facilities, logistics, and operational needs;
3. pitch Forge as the workforce and service-operations marketplace;
4. pitch the Forge app as the employer-intake, qualification, matching, scheduling, evidence, CRM, and reporting system;
5. develop an approved Flex referral/channel relationship for eligible Forge business owners;
6. explore a separate workforce or vendor-capacity conversation for Flex's own defined operational needs; and
7. preserve human approvals for external communication, contracts, staffing commitments, regulated activity, money movement, and production changes.

The operating formula is:

**60 software agents → verified account intelligence → human-approved outreach → scoped discovery → qualified worker/provider capacity → controlled pilot → evidence → recurring enterprise account**

Forge can evaluate a defined need for:

- HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and facilities maintenance;
- cleaning, turnover, landscaping, hardscape, and property services;

Forge must not promise personnel until availability, qualifications, classification, insurance, safety, licensing, rate, geography, schedule, and contracting structure have been verified.

### Offer B — Forge app and Nexus operating layer

The app pitch is:

> Forge gives employers one controlled place to submit workforce/service demand, set qualification requirements, review matched providers, schedule work, track documents and status, capture proof of completion, manage follow-up, and view performance. Nexus coordinates the internal agent, CRM, QA, security, and reporting workflows behind that experience.

A pilot should begin with one location, one trade/service category, or one recurring bottleneck.

## 8. Flex-specific strategy

Keep three opportunities separate:

### 8.1 Approved Flex referral channel

Apply through the formal Flex partner process. Forge may refer eligible business owners only under approved terms and using the official referral link. Flex controls application, underwriting, approval, onboarding, product terms, and support.

Forge must not claim to be Flex, a bank, a lender, a credit decision-maker, or an authorized Flex representative before a signed agreement permits the exact language.

### 8.2 Forge distribution value for Flex

Forge can potentially introduce qualified construction, wholesale, facilities, property, logistics, and other middle-market owners who fit Flex's current target segment. Referral value must be measured by consented, qualified, activated accounts—not raw names.

### 8.3 Flex operational workforce/vendor discussion

Separately ask whether Flex has defined needs involving office buildout, facilities, maintenance, event support, field operations, logistics, vendor coordination, recruiting support, or other blue-collar/service capacity. Do not assume such needs exist. Route the inquiry to the correct operations, procurement, facilities, people, or vendor-management owner.

## 9. Outreach sequence

### Touch 1 — Business email or official contact form

Goal: secure a 20- to 30-minute discovery call around one specific operational bottleneck.

The email must:

- state what Forge is building without overstating current capacity;
- identify the demand hypothesis as a reason for outreach, not a known fact;
- offer a controlled pilot;
- pitch both workforce/service coordination and the app;
- avoid pricing until scope exists;
- avoid claims of existing partnership, vendor approval, licensing, or worker availability;
- include the standard legal signature; and
- remain marked not sent until an authorized connector confirms success.

### Touch 2 — Business phone follow-up

Ask for the role responsible for operations, procurement, workforce planning, facilities, construction, field services, vendor management, or partnerships.

Call opener:

> “This is Andrew with Forge. We are building a controlled workforce and service-operations platform. I am not calling to promise generic labor. I am trying to identify one real bottleneck—one region, trade, recurring service, or project need—where a qualified-provider pilot and app-based workflow may be useful. Who owns that process?”

### Touch 3 — Evidence-based follow-up

Send a one-page pilot outline or app demonstration relevant to the confirmed need. Do not send an unrelated generic deck.

### Touch 4 — Pilot proposal

Define:

- scope and exclusions;
- location;
- required licenses/certifications;
- worker/provider model;
- insurance and safety requirements;
- schedule and volume;
- pricing assumptions;
- Forge fee and payment terms;
- evidence and reporting;
- change control;
- data handling;
- escalation; and
- renewal/termination.

## 10. Workforce compliance gate

Before Forge supplies or coordinates any worker or provider, determine the lawful commercial model:

- marketplace referral;
- subcontractor/vendor coordination;
- licensed contractor relationship;
- staffing agency;
- labor contractor;
- employer of record;
- direct employment; or
- another counsel-approved structure.

The required review may include:

- state contractor and staffing/labor rules;
- employee-versus-independent-contractor classification;
- workers' compensation;
- general and professional liability;
- auto and umbrella coverage;
- payroll, wage-hour, overtime, meal/rest, prevailing-wage, union, and certified-payroll requirements;
- I-9 and tax documentation;
- background checks and Fair Credit Reporting Act processes where applicable;
- occupational licensing and certifications;
- OSHA and jobsite safety;
- client procurement and vendor onboarding;
- data privacy and retention;
- non-discrimination and accessibility; and
- incident, injury, dispute, and termination procedures.

No agent may bypass this gate because a client says the need is urgent.
